## 2025-02-23 - [Optimize NumPy Top-K Selection with argpartition]
**Learning:** `np.argsort` has O(N log N) complexity, causing performance bottlenecks during top-K candidate selection in large vector search indices (e.g., K3 MRL Indexer). For top-K selection without a full sort, `np.argpartition` provides O(N) complexity. In benchmarks, switching from `argsort` to `argpartition` for K=75 out of 100,000 vectors reduced the execution time from ~4.0ms down to ~0.36ms (a 10x+ improvement).
**Action:** When extracting top-K candidates from large NumPy arrays (e.g., scoring matrices, similarity calculations), always prioritize `np.argpartition` followed by sorting just the selected partition, rather than using `np.argsort` on the entire array.

## 2025-02-23 - [Optimize Cosine Similarity via Einsum/Raw Dot Product]
**Learning:** During MatryoshkaIndexer search reranking (and initial candidate selection when caching is absent), the code performs cosine similarity using `m_norm = m / (np.linalg.norm(m, axis=1, keepdims=True) + 1e-9)`. This allocates massive intermediate NxD matrices. By refactoring to compute a raw dot product first (`np.dot(m, q)`) and then scaling by the 1D magnitudes (`np.sqrt(np.einsum('ij,ij->i', m, m) * np.dot(q, q))`), we avoid NxD allocation inside the hot loop and reduce the execution time by ~50-80% (e.g., from ~84ms to ~38ms for small batches, and 32ms to 4ms for larger matrices in benchmarks).
**Action:** When computing batched cosine similarity with NumPy in hot loops, use `np.einsum` for squared norms and raw `np.dot` products instead of fully normalizing the matrix prior to the dot product.
